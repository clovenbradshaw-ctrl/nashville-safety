=== PEDESTRIAN INFRASTRUCTURE DEFICITS ===

Walk Bike Nashville — Impossible Crossings report:
URL: https://www.walkbikenashville.org/impossible_crossings
Key verbatim: "80% of all pedestrians killed in Nashville are killed on state-controlled arterial streets with four or more lanes, speed limits over 30 mph and few safe pedestrian crossings."
The Welshwood Drive / Nolensville Pike intersection alone had 7 people killed since 2010.

Walk Bike Nashville — framing on sidewalk deficit:
"the city is seventy years behind on building sidewalks because as residential development sprawled outwards post-WWII, the city was designed for cars instead of people."

=== COVID EFFECT ON NASHVILLE TRAFFIC ===

TDOT monitoring: Tennessee roadway volumes dropped roughly 40% in April 2020 vs. 2019.
By September 2020 congestion returned to inner loops. By late 2020/early 2021, Nashville-area traffic ~5-10% below 2019 levels.

PMC study: "Mobility Dynamics amid COVID-19 with a Case Study in Tennessee" (2023)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10149353/
Confirms volume drop pattern.

Gap: No Nashville-specific data on whether speeds increased during low-volume COVID periods.

=== NATIONAL VS NASHVILLE COMPARISON ===

National: U.S. pedestrian fatalities rose from ~5,997 (2016) to 7,737 (2022) — approximately 29% increase.
GHSA: pedestrian fatalities increased "at a pace nine times higher than population growth" between first halves of 2019 and 2022.
URL: https://www.ghsa.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/2023%20Pedestrian%20Traffic%20Fatalities%20by%20State.pdf

Nashville: Deaths rose from 17 (2016) to 39 (2020) to 49 (2022) — approximately 188% increase over 2016-2022 window.
Nashville per-capita rate approximately 5.51 per 100,000 — exceeds national average and most peer Sun Belt cities except Houston and Dallas.

Key comparison: Nashville's 188% increase vs national 29% increase over same window. Nashville's surge far outpaces national trend, requiring a Nashville-specific explanation. However Sun Belt cities with arterial-heavy road networks tracked similar above-average surges — infrastructure/design also competes as explanation.

Tennessee statewide 2023: GHSA put TN at 173 pedestrian fatalities in 2023, rate of 1.36 per 100,000 vs national ~1.10-1.20 for comparable period.
URL: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813727

=== KEY NASHVILLE INVESTIGATIONS ===

Nashville Banner (November 18, 2024): "Nashville traffic fatalities on the rise amid police enforcement decline."
URL: https://nashvillebanner.com/2024/11/18/nashville-police-mostly-stopped-pulling-cars-over-but-some-councilmembers-want-to-reverse-the-trend/
Key verbatim: traffic stops declined 91% from 2015 to 2023; "between 2018 and 2023, traffic fatalities increased by 75 percent as enforcement fell." Notes Policing Project study influenced MNPD's shift. Frames as correlation, not mechanism.
NOTE: This is the strongest press investigation framing MNPD enforcement decline as proximate cause.

WKRN (2023/2024): "Road design, fewer traffic stops contribute to Nashville road deaths, expert says."
URL: https://www.wkrn.com/news/local-news/nashville/expert-says-road-design-fewer-traffic-stops-contribute-to-nashville-road-deaths/
Key finding: Expert explicitly names BOTH road design AND reduced enforcement as contributing — frames them as complementary, not competing.

Metro Council resolution (2025): enforcement "should include not only traffic stops and police presence to deter speeders, but also systemic improvements in street design, engineering, and policy."

INFRASTRUCTURE COMPETITION WITH ENFORCEMENT HYPOTHESIS:
Walk Bike Nashville verbatim: "80% of all pedestrians killed in Nashville are killed on state-controlled arterial streets with four or more lanes, speed limits over 30 mph and few safe pedestrian crossings."
This is the strongest competing explanation: these infrastructure deficits predate the enforcement collapse AND the death surge. The deficits set the stage; the enforcement collapse may have removed the last active deterrent on those streets.
